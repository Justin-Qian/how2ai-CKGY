The paper studies the problem of linear increase in computational costs and activation memory as the hidden layer width grows in standard transformer architectures. It proposes Sparse Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures to decouple model size from computational cost. The study introduces PEER (Parameter Efficient Expert Retrieval), a design that uses a product key technique to retrieve from over a million tiny experts. Compared to other methods, it demonstrates superior performance-compute trade-off. It allows for scaling of transformer models while maintaining computational efficiency. The paper further investigates the under-explored case of many tiny experts and demonstrates that a learned index structure can efficiently route to over a million experts.
